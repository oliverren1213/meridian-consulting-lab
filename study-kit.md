# Meridian Consulting Lab — Working kit

An eight-week, two-person consulting learning programme. Proposed independent student initiative connecting HEC–Bocconi and HKU. No university or club endorsement is implied.

## The weekly commitment

Each person: 45 minutes of preparation, a 60-minute partner session, 60–90 minutes on the shared project, and 15–30 minutes on internship preparation. Total: about 3–4 hours. Alternate who interviews first. Both people practise the full toolkit.

Agree a recurring meeting yourself; this kit does not create calendar events. Use Europe/Rome or Europe/Paris and Asia/Hong_Kong when scheduling, so daylight saving is handled correctly.

If a week is overloaded, use a 90-minute minimum: 20 minutes preparing, 40 minutes working together and 30 minutes producing one small research output. Move the programme back one week rather than quietly skipping the skill. Review usefulness and workload after week four.

## Eight-week roadmap

| Week | Skill | Research output | Career action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structure a problem | Problem statement and issue tree | Five verified target roles |
| 2 | Size a market | Assumptions, sizing range, interview guide | Identify one skill gap |
| 3 | Understand profitability | Unit economics and first interviews | Prepare a targeted practitioner conversation |
| 4 | Read customers and competition | Themes, comparison table, midpoint review | Ask for a case review and revise one CV bullet |
| 5 | Choose a market | Options and a provisional recommendation | Tailor an application to a real opening |
| 6 | Turn data into an insight | One decision-focused chart and source audit | Practise a two-minute project story |
| 7 | Make a recommendation | Draft research deck and executive summary | Prepare two truthful fit-interview stories |
| 8 | Defend your work | Final deck, portfolio summary, next practice priority | Review applications and interview feedback |

These actions are a cadence, not a reason to delay a live application. Check deadlines and eligibility from the start.

## One-page problem statement

Who must make a decision?
What exactly is the decision?
What would a useful answer enable?
What is in scope: user, location, product and period?
What is out of scope?
What are the main hypotheses?
What information would support or weaken each hypothesis?
What is the simplest useful deliverable?

## Exploratory interview guide

Before the interview, explain the student research purpose, ask permission to take notes, say how the notes will be used and when identifying information will be removed. Participation is voluntary. Do not collect bank credentials, account statements or unnecessary personal financial details.

1. Tell me about the last time you transferred money for living expenses.
2. What were you trying to do, and what steps did you take?
3. Which alternatives did you consider? Why did you choose this one?
4. What was confusing, costly or inconvenient, if anything?
5. What would make you consider changing how you do this?
6. What makes you trust or distrust a new provider?

Avoid assuming everyone has a problem or asking “Would you use our amazing cheaper app?” Separate what happened from hypothetical intentions.

## First research project

Proposed question: what should a cross-border money tool solve for international students?

Start with one segment in Milan. Aim for 6–8 adult student interviews in Milan and 2–4 in Hong Kong only if that comparison helps. Compare 4–6 products using the same transfer scenario and dated public information. If recruitment is difficult, narrow to one city.

This is a small exploratory convenience sample; never turn the interview percentages into population-level claims. No client is assumed. The output is independent research, not financial advice or evidence of client impact.

### Suggested final deck

1. Research question and decision
2. Executive recommendation
3. Scope, method and limitations
4. Customer problem and observed behaviour
5. Important differences between users
6. Competitive alternatives
7. Economics or a sizing range, if evidence supports it
8. Comparison of options
9. Recommended experiment
10. Risks and conditions that could change the answer
11. Contributions and learning
12. Sources and calculation appendix

Combine slides where useful. The target is 10–12 readable slides, not an obligation to fill every slot. Publish anonymised material only within participant consent and source usage rights.

## Outreach draft

Hi [Name],

I’m a [verified programme and year] student exploring [specific type of work]. I’m currently working on an independent research project about [question]. Your experience in [specific, verified area] caught my attention.

Would you have 15 minutes to share what a junior team member actually does and which skills matter most? I’d particularly value your perspective on [one specific question].

Thank you,
[Name]

Only use accurate, relevant details. Send individually, and respect a non-response. This is a conversation request, not an assumed referral.

## CV and interview evidence

Write only after the work exists. A possible structure is:

“Co-developed an independent study of [question], using [methods actually completed] to compare [options] and present [defensible recommendation].”

Add interview counts or other figures only when verified. Do not describe this as client consulting, university-backed work or a commercial result unless that is true. Be ready to explain your own contribution and how you used tools.

## Where to learn next

Use firms’ official careers pages for their current interview formats and official practice cases. Your own courses and any training materials you are authorised to use can support the methods. Never copy confidential club or client materials into this project.

The eight mini-cases on the website are original fictional exercises. They introduce the skills; they do not replace full-length case interviews. After week four, ask a willing experienced peer to review an attempt and use their feedback to adjust the next cycle.
